abstract: |-
  Deep neural networks can fit datasets with noisy labels, but not all types of label
  noise are equally harmful. While symmetric noise is largely unstructured and relatively
  easy to mitigate, asymmetric and instance-dependent noise introduce biased supervision
  patterns that can be learned as true signal. Relabeling and self-training pipelines can par-
  tially mitigate noise by refining labels using model predictions, but they often exacerbate
  bias by reinforcing structured error patterns. In a different direction, injecting symmetric
  noise globally has been explored as a regularization strategy; however, its indiscrimi-
  nate application does not address the structured bias induced by model predictions. In
  this paper, we propose Bias-Breaking Relabeling (BBR), a model-agnostic framework
  for robust learning under structured label noise. Instead of applying symmetric noise
  as a global regularizer or replacing suspected noisy labels with model predictions, BBR
  assigns random labels to detected noisy samples, converting structured noise into approx-
  imately symmetric noise. This transformation reduces alignment with class semantics,
  making corrupted labels easier to identify and suppress during training. In effect, BBR
  induces an effective corruption dominated by approximately symmetric noise, removing
  structured bias while ensuring that detection errors introduce only unstructured pertur-
  bations. Unlike conventional approaches that seek to improve label correctness, BBR
  reduces the learnability of incorrect labels by destroying their structure. Across syn-
  thetic (i.e., symmetric, asymmetric, and instance-dependent) and real-world label noise
  settings, we show that BBR successfully mitigates performance degradation across mul-
  tiple noisy-label learning methods.
