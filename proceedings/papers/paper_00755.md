abstract: |-
  Remote photoplethysmography (rPPG) enables non-contact vital-sign
  measurement using cameras, but deep learning rPPG models trained on one
  dataset often fail on another due to variation in illumination, motion,
  skin appearance, and camera response. We identify two reasons for this
  cross-domain failure: feature-scale dispersion destabilising early
  representations; and spectral heterogeneity that forces a trade-off
  between accurate pulse recovery and motion robustness. Furthermore,
  selecting and comparing models by mean error can leave rare catastrophic
  windows undetected. We propose to treat cross-domain rPPG extraction as a
  reliability-aware decision problem in which each window can be predicted,
  routed between experts, blended, or rejected according to estimated
  risk. The solution is called DARE-Net (Domain-Aware Robust Expert
  Network) with a Mamba-based dual-expert backbone. It uses a
  Stability-Aware Fusion Stem to bound early feature energy and a
  Domain-Aware Robust Expert module using two different experts to deal with
  clean signals and motion-corrupted signals, respectively. Inter-expert
  disagreement provides a natural signal for routing and selective
  prediction. Validated on cross-domain benchmarks, DARE-Net improves the
  cross-domain generalizability as evidenced in higher accuracies for heart
  rate predictions in MMPD and VIPL. Under a subject-aware out-of-fold
  protocol, the reliability layer further controls tail risk: on the
  motion-heavy MMPD, abstaining on low-confidence windows roughly halves the
  rate of catastrophic errors (heart-rate error \(>10\)\,bpm) on the
  retained windows. Code is publicly available at https://github.com/Junjie31/DARENET.
