abstract: |-
  Dense annotation remains a fundamental bottleneck in medical image pretraining for segmentation, yet prevailing self-supervised methods either reconstruct low-level appearance or rely on task-specific anatomical priors to achieve structural awareness. Both paradigms limit transferability to new organs, modalities, and structural targets. To this end, we introduce TASP, a task-agnostic structural pretraining framework that learns segmentation-ready dense representations from unlabeled medical images without target masks or handcrafted anatomical rules.

  TASP addresses these limitations through three tightly coupled mechanisms rather than a simple stack of independent modules. A spatial variational prototypical bottleneck abstracts reusable topology-preserving primitives beyond task-specific anatomical priors; a structure-dominant, appearance-compensated reconstruction objective shifts pretraining from low-level texture reconstruction toward structure-aware layout recovery; and a teacher-guided adaptive masking strategy feeds back gradient saliency, prototype entropy, and teacher-student structural disagreement to focus subsequent learning on structurally informative regions. In this way, TASP forms a closed-loop structural discovery process that progressively emphasizes high-information structural discrepancies.We evaluate TASP on coronary vessel, colonoscopy polyp, and fundus optic disc/cup segmentation. Experimental results show that TASP consistently outperforms strong generic and medical self-supervised baselines, demonstrating target-agnostic structure modeling as an effective and scalable route toward reusable medical image pretraining.
