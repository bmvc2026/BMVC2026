abstract: |-
  Class-attention knowledge distillation transfers class-specific spatial knowledge from a teacher to a student, typically by aligning class activation maps (CAMs). However, most CAM-based methods still aggregate all class-wise CAM losses under a uniform objective, leaving the different optimization roles of target-class and non-target-class supervision insufficiently explored. Target-class CAMs are closely related to discriminative learning for the ground-truth category, whereas non-target CAMs provide auxiliary cross-class guidance whose usefulness may vary across training states.
  To this end, we introduce Gradient-Guided Role-Aware CAM Distillation (GRCD), a CAM distillation framework that separates supervision by role and coordinates it using gradient compatibility. GRCD decomposes CAM alignment into target-class CAM (TCAM) and non-target-class CAM (NCAM) objectives, keeps TCAM as a stable anchor, and adaptively weights NCAM according to its gradient compatibility with TCAM through a smooth bounded controller. In this way, GRCD avoids static supervision allocation while retaining useful non-target relational cues when they are optimization-compatible. Experiments on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet under both homogeneous and heterogeneous teacher--student settings show that GRCD consistently improves CAM-based distillation baselines, validating the effectiveness of state-dependent coordination between target and non-target CAM supervision.
