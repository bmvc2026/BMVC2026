abstract: 'Novel view synthesis from sparse, uncalibrated outdoor images is challenging due to three key factors: sparse viewpoints, unknown camera poses, and varying illumination. Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting methods address the first two challenges, but reconstruction quality remains sensitive to inconsistent global illumination. In contrast, in-the-wild Gaussian Splatting methods handle illumination variation but require dense, pose-calibrated inputs. To this end, we propose a novel Illumination Adaptation Module (IAM) to mitigate the assumption of globally consistent illumination in feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting. IAM generates improved spherical harmonic parameters under varying illumination conditions, which are crucial for subsequent geometry refinement. Experiments show that our method outperforms both feed-forward baseline, AnySplat and in-the-wild baseline, WildGaussians, improving PSNR by +2.29 dB and +3.24 dB respectively.'
