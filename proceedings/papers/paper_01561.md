abstract: |-
  We propose a novel GAN-based architecture for one-to-many image-to-image translation and use it to generate image pairs for
  training retrieval models. Our motivating application is shoeprint image retrieval: matching low-quality crime scene shoe impressions (shoemarks) against high-quality reference impressions (shoeprints). Despite footwear marks being the most prevalent crime scene evidence, matching them remains a labour-intensive and subjective task. Automating this process is hindered by the lack of large public paired datasets. We address this data deficit by framing shoemark generation as a one-to-many translation problem, conditioning the translation on a style vector to generate multiple varied shoemarks from a single shoeprint. Because shoemarks are often partial or degraded, the resulting asymmetry in semantic information prevents the use of cycle regularisation; instead, we adapt shortest path regularisation by parameterising the translation path via the style vector to ensure translation accuracy. Furthermore, extracting a style vector from a real shoemark enables example-guided translation. We present a synthetic data generation pipeline incorporating our GAN to train shoeprint image retrieval models. In experiments across multiple datasets, our GAN outperforms a comparable multimodal translation model (MUNIT), and our trained retrieval model achieves state-of-the-art results on real-world footwear marks.
