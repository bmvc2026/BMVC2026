abstract: |-
  Real-world vision is dynamic and scene-rich, while EEG and fMRI resolve it at com-
  plementary temporal and spatial scales. Most neural decoding systems, however, align
  one modality in isolation and mostly against static images or short clips. We study the
  synchronized case: EEG and fMRI are recorded during the same long-form naturalis-
  tic viewing, giving a shared stimulus but also recurring narrative context and many se-
  mantically similar candidates. We present BrainBind, a framework that maps synchro-
  nized EEG and fMRI into frozen long-form video embedding spaces and treats those
  spaces as common visual-semantic references, not literal neural codes. On CineBrain,
  a five-seed Recall@K 2×4 matrix across two video anchors and four system configura-
  tions shows distinct retrieval profiles. With Omni, fMRI organizes the full candidate list
  most strongly (MRR = 23.82, nDCG@10 = 25.31), while Joint-Dual gives the highest
  mean label-aware R@1 (13.52%) with a distinct rank-wide profile. The complementary
  episode-disjoint evaluation quantifies the contribution of repeated stimulus identity and
  narrative context to the random split. Across these experiments, proxy choice and sys-
  tem configuration produce distinct top-rank and rank-wide retrieval profiles within the
  present synchronized dataset.
