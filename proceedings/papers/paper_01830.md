abstract: |-
  Decoding visual perception from electroencephalography (EEG) offers a scalable and non-invasive path to brain-computer interfaces, yet it remains hindered by a persistent challenge: pronounced inter-subject variability, which severely limits the model’s ability to generalize to unseen data and degrades cross-subject generalization. Most existing methods rely on subject-specific components or require per-subject fine-tuning, limiting scalability to unseen individuals and unseen visual categories. We address both challenges simultaneously under a zero-shot protocol where training and test visual categories are entirely disjoint, drawn from common semantic meta-categories, with no
  subject-specific adaptation permitted at inference.

  We propose GRACE (Generalizable Residual Autoencoder for Cross-subject EEG Decoding), a masked autoencoder pretraining framework that learns a disentangled EEG representation space through residual decomposition. Built on the LaBraM architecture with symmetric masked reconstruction, GRACE introduces a Subject-Invariant Feature Extraction module that factorizes encoder representations into a subject-invariant component and a subject-specific residual. Disentanglement is enforced by three complementary objectives: an adversarial loss preventing subject identity from leaking into subject-invariant features, a classification loss anchoring to subject identity, and an orthogonality penalty.

  Evaluated on the THINGS-EEG2 and Alljoined-1.6M benchmarks under the cross-subject zero-shot protocol, GRACE outperforms prior methods across both low-level perceptual and high-level semantic metrics, demonstrating that explicit residual decomposition with orthogonality regularization substantially closes the gap between subject-specific and subject-agnostic EEG visual decoding.
