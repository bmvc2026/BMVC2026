abstract: |-
  Robust semantic segmentation in adverse weather conditions is essential for safety-critical autonomous systems. Current approaches generally follow two paradigms: sequential restore-then-segment pipelines, which introduce significant latency and prioritize pixel-level restoration over semantic discriminability, and domain adaptation techniques, which often struggle with representation bottlenecks and computational complexity. To tackle these challenges, we introduce LiDSeg, a framework that leverages reconstruction-informed guidance to improve segmentation under adverse weather. LiDSeg adopts an asymmetric training strategy in which a lightweight guidance encoder is jointly optimized with the segmentation branch under a combined segmentation and auxiliary reconstruction objective, encouraging the learning of features that are robust to weather corruptions. At inference, the reconstruction decoder is discarded to reduce computational overhead, while the guidance encoder is retained to extract weather-robust semantic features. A lightweight gated fusion (LGF) module then dynamically integrates these guidance features into the main segmentation branch, preserving clear-weather accuracy while substantially improving adverse-weather robustness.
  Experiments across multiple datasets show that LiDSeg achieves a favorable balance between robustness and computational efficiency. On Foggy Cityscapes, it limits the clear-to-dense-fog mIoU degradation to only $2.5$--$2.8$ percentage points, reducing the baseline performance drop by more than $5\times$. On the official ACDC test server, LiDSeg-PIDNet reaches $75.07\%$ pooled all-condition mIoU, comparable to the supervised HRNet-W48 result of $75.0\%$, while remaining substantially smaller. The gains also generalize in a zero-shot setting to Foggy Driving and Foggy Zurich, where LiDSeg uses no real-fog images for training. More importantly, this robustness is achieved through an adaptable, plug-and-play design that adds only $1.9$--$3.1$M parameters across several models and costs no inference-time reconstruction overhead. The resulting models remain highly compact, ranging from $5.2$M to $39.5$M total parameters, and deliver real-time inference on resource-constrained edge devices such as the NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin. Code is available at \footnote{\url{https://github.com/yobsanb/LiDSeg}}.
