abstract: |-
  Image-level evaluation in subject-driven generation systematically misses structural
  failures: a generation can drop a distinctive structural component while still scoring
  near the maximum of every tested identity metric. We first identify this blind zone
  on DreamBench, where human judgments and all tested identity metrics consistently
  disagree on a small set of structurally distinctive subjects. We then characterize it sys-
  tematically on Non-canonical Bench, a targeted challenge set of 25 freely designed,
  multi-component subjects that lack canonical real-world category templates (626 im-
  ages, 418 densely human-annotated). We introduce Structural Component Retention
  (SCR), a per-component verdict vector automated by a VLM-as-judge, as a comple-
  mentary diagnostic that localizes which component drives the failure. Because the VLM
  judge is itself imperfect, we decompose its agreement with humans using a 3×3 reliabil-
  ity framework and isolate Type-C precision over-credit, a failure mode that captures
  the systematic over-credit shared across identity metrics. On all five evaluated metrics,
  per-method Type-C rates predict metric-specific bias, a relationship all four VLM judges
  reproduce. This suggests Type-C rate is an image-level signal of content that passes al-
  gorithmic similarity yet fails human structural verification, rather than an artifact of any
  particular judge. As with HEIM and Otani et al. in T2I evaluation, what we contribute
  is an evaluation instrument rather than an intervention in the models being evaluated.
  Finally, we discuss how SCR could inform future improvements to generation. Annota-
  tions, verdicts and code: https://github.com/HuaiyuanHe/Structural-
  Blind-Zone-SDG.
