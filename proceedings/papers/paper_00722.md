abstract: |-
  Dynamic street-scene reconstruction is essential for autonomous driving, as it provides spatial-temporal representations for perception, simulation, and planning.
  Recent 4D Gaussian Splatting methods achieve efficient rendering and dynamic scene modeling, but most of them remain black-box optimization systems driven by photometric losses and heuristic Gaussian updates.
  As a result, it is difficult to explain why reconstruction errors occur, which factors dominate failure regions, and how the Gaussian structure should be corrected.
  Moreover, adding interpretability may introduce extra optimization burden and degrade reconstruction quality.

  To address these issues, we propose IESC-4DGS, a plug-and-play interpretable and self-corrective framework for dynamic street-scene 4D Gaussian reconstruction.
  Our framework consists of several newly designed modules: Sparse Diagnostic Attribution (SDA) identifies sparse high-residual regions and decomposes errors into motion, geometry, visibility, and static-dynamic assignment factors; Dynamic Diagnostic Prototype Memory (DPM) and Prototype Online Purification (POP) allow human-readable diagnostic prototypes to automatically emerge, evolve, and be purified from long-term optimization behavior without predefined templates; Prototype-Guided Correction Gate (PGCG) and Render-Safe Guard (RSG) convert matched prototypes into render-safe correction signals; and Lightweight Structural Modulation (LSM) transforms these signals into lightweight structural modulation for Gaussian densification, pruning, opacity adjustment, and scale regularization.
  Together, these modules form a closed-loop diagnosis-memory-correction framework.

  Experiments show IESC-4DGS provides human-readable explanations of failure modes, prototype evolution, and correction behavior, while maintaining competitive reconstruction quality and rendering efficiency.
  The results demonstrate the proposed framework bridges diagnostic interpretability and high-performance 4DGS reconstruction.
