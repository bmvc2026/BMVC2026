abstract: |-
  Recent radar-based object detection methods exploit diverse radar representations, including point clouds, range--Doppler--azimuth tensors, and range--Doppler (RD) maps.
  RD representations preserve low-level amplitude and phase information before angle formation, but angular cues are encoded implicitly in antenna-channel phase relationships without an explicit angle dimension.
  Existing RD-based detectors typically extract features in RD space and infer range--azimuth structure only at the final prediction stage, which can limit spatial reasoning for object detection.
  In this paper, we propose A$^2$-RadNet, an angle-aware representation learning framework that maps complex RD inputs to a structured Range--Pseudo-Angle (RPA) representation before backbone processing.
  The proposed Angle-Aware Encoder (AAE) exploits antenna-, transmit-channel-, and Doppler-dependent structures to generate learned pseudo-angle responses, rather than forming calibrated azimuth bins by angle FFT or beamforming.
  AAE is instantiated with Doppler-Structured and Regular-Antenna variants to handle different radar configurations.
  By reorganizing angle-related cues into a range--pseudo-angle layout, A$^2$-RadNet enables the backbone to extract features in a range--angle-like space while training the final outputs with RA-space supervision.
  Experiments on controlled Doppler-shifted MIMO data and real radar benchmarks demonstrate improved angle-aware representation learning and detection performance, while ablation studies validate the effectiveness of AAE and the RPA representation.
