abstract: 'Cross-modal remote sensing image retrieval (RS-CMIR) requires representations that are simultaneously modality-invariant and semantically discriminative, despite the profound physical gap between heterogeneous sensors such as Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) and multispectral imagery. Recent JEPA-based Predictive self-supervised approaches such as X-JEPA formulate cross-modal alignment as a deterministic latent forecasting task, implicitly assuming uniform patch predictability and leaving cross-modal distributional alignment unaddressed. We propose CAPE-JEPA, a probabilistic cross-modal Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture with three contributions: (i) a cross attentional Gaussian predictor outputting per-patch parameters $(\mu, \sigma^{2})$ conditioned on opposite-modality context, trained via negative log-likelihood to model inherent cross-sensor prediction uncertainty; (ii) adversarial distributional regularization combining unimodal isotropic Gaussian regularization with a novel cross-modal adversarial variant that aligns modality distributions via worst-case Epps-Pulley characteristic function tests without explicit sample pairing; and (iii) in-training contrastive learning with attention-pooled heads for scene-level discriminative grounding. Evaluated on BigEarthNet-14K (BEN-14K) and fMoW across SAR, multispectral, and RGB modalities, CAPE-JEPA surpasses X-JEPA by up to $\mathbf{13.74\text{ points}}$ on BEN-14K and $\mathbf{15.59\text{ points}}$ on fMoW, outperforming all baselines including SkySense and CROMA across both unimodal and cross-modal directions while using fewer parameters (407.30M vs. 432.72M).'
