abstract: |-
  Foundation Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP exhibit strong generalization capabilities due to large-scale pretraining on diverse image-text pairs. Recent fewshot adaptation approaches efficiently adapt these models to downstream datasets using
  parameter-efficient finetuning strategies like prompt tuning. However, existing methods are typically evaluated on standard natural image benchmarks, which closely align with web-scale pretraining data. In real-world applications, target datasets often exhibit
  significant distribution shifts in visual appearance as well as class semantics, making alignment between visual and textual representations significantly more challenging under limited supervision. In this work, we propose DMPT (Distributional Multi-Prompt
  Tuning), a novel framework for adapting CLIP to few-shot settings with severe distribution shifts. We observe that under such challenging settings, class representations often become fragmented into multiple modes in the embedding space, while limited training
  samples make prompt learning highly prone to overfitting. To address these challenges, DMPT introduces multiple learnable prompts per class to better model multimodal class distributions. Additionally, instead of learning prompts deterministically, we represent
  the prompt parameters using learnable Gaussian distributions, enabling stochastic exploration of the prompt space and improving generalization in low-data regimes. Extensive experiments on multiple challenging benchmarks demonstrate that DMPT consistently
  outperforms state-of-the-art adaptation methods, achieving 3.19% absolute improvement in the challenging 1-shot setting while exhibiting improved robustness and lower sensitivity to training sample selection.
