abstract: |-
  Cross-platform outdoor 3D visual grounding (3DVG) requires localizing language-referred targets across heterogeneous robotic platforms with substantially different viewpoints, visibility patterns, and sensing configurations. Such cross-platform variation introduces severe viewpoint-conditioned semantic ambiguity, making existing multimodal fusion methods fragile under platform shift. Most existing approaches directly fuse language, image, and point-cloud features into a shared representation space, implicitly assuming stable cross-modal alignment across platforms. However, semantic evidence itself changes with viewpoint variation, making direct multimodal fusion insufficient for reliable grounding.
  In this work, we reformulate cross-platform outdoor 3DVG as a semantic-geometric hypothesis verification problem. Instead of directly regressing 3D targets from fused multimodal features, our method first generates metric-scale 3D hypotheses and then verifies whether they are supported by current-view language-conditioned semantic evidence. Based on this formulation, we propose Semantic-Geometric Verification (SGV), which instantiates semantic verification at both inference and training stages. SGV-I selects semantically supported 3D hypotheses during inference, while SGV-T performs semantic admission control on target-platform pseudo-labels before memory ensembling during cross-platform self-training.
  Experiments on the 3EED multi-platform outdoor 3DVG benchmark demonstrate that SGV consistently improves cross-platform grounding robustness. SGV-I improves average Acc@50 by 31.73 percentage points in same-platform settings, while the full SGV-I + ST3D++ + SGV-T pipeline improves average Acc@50 by 38.80 percentage points in cross-platform settings.
