abstract: |-
  Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) achieve strong performance in image captioning through global image-text alignment, yet their ability to generate fine-grained, physically grounded, and object-level captions remain limited.
  Real-world objects have complex part structures that require accurate part-level attention, correct relational connectivity, and object-level semantic coherence.
  To address these limitations, we propose \textbf{mLVLM}, a multiple-constrained LVLM that extends the standard training objective with three complementary energy terms: part-alignment energy, which anchors attention to semantically meaningful parts; structural-connectivity energy, which enforces physically grounded part-to-part relations via spring mechanics and graph supervision; and caption-context consistency energy, which reduces semantic drift at the object level.
  Part-level supervision is obtained from dataset annotations and off-the-shelf detectors, and auxiliary training heads are removed at inference, leaving the backbone architecture and computational cost unchanged.
  Experiments on Valves, CUB-Birds, and Stanford Cars show that mLVLM consistently improves fine-grained captioning and recognition over strong LVLM baselines, with the clearest gains on objects whose identity depends on connected parts and structural detail.
