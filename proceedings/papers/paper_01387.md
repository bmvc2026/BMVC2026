abstract: |-
  Automatic digitization of hand-drawn sketches into editable formats is crucial for modern document workflows and LaTeX integration. Converting sketches into executable TikZ code provides a structured representation of diagrams, but remains challenging due to noisy and ambiguous inputs, which hinder reliable interpretation and limit the effectiveness of token-level supervision for ensuring global diagram validity.
  To address these challenges, we propose ${Sketch2TikZ}$, a reinforcement learning framework that optimizes sketch-to-code generation via a sequential multi-reward strategy, combining Code-Structure ($R_{cs}$), OCR ($R_{ocr}$), CleanDIFT ($R_{cd}$), and Self-Similarity ($R_{ss}$) rewards to enforce textual, visual and structural correctness. We evaluate Sketch2TikZ on the ${SkeTikZ}$ and ${FlowChart-3B}$ benchmarks using metrics including compilation success rate (CSR), image similarity, code similarity,
  and character similarity. Our method achieves near-perfect compilation success and improves visual and textual alignment over strong open-source baselines, while remaining competitive with proprietary GPT-based vision–language models. Extensive ablations demonstrate that curriculum-based reward scheduling is critical for reducing reward interference, stabilizing training and mitigating proxy over-optimization. Overall, our results highlight the effectiveness of reference-aware, multi-objective reinforcement learning for structured diagram generation from noisy sketches.
