abstract: |-
  Test-Time Prompt Tuning (TPT) adapts Vision-Language Models (VLMs) at inference without any labelled target data, yet its standard entropy-based objective treats every test sample identically: it sharpens predictions indiscriminately, producing overconfident errors on ambiguous or out-of-distribution inputs and degrading confidence calibration.
  We present **CR-TPT** (**C**ontinuously **R**outed Test-Time Prompt Tuning), a calibration-aware framework that replaces this one-size-fits-all strategy with two complementary objectives whose relative contribution is continuously regulated by a per-sample routing signal derived from an online memory. An *Euclidean Knowledge Base* (EKB) accumulates high-confidence past predictions and constructs entropy-weighted, topology-smoothed supervision targets through nearest-neighbour retrieval. The normalised entropy of the retrieved target then drives a *continuous routing gate* ϕ ∈ [0, 1]: when memory retrieval is confident (low retrieval entropy), ϕ routes the update toward Objective A, a knowledge-anchored sharpening loss; when retrieval is uncertain (high retrieval entropy), ϕ routes toward Objective B, a confidence-capped distillation loss that prevents overconfident collapse. A final confidence-weighted inference gate combines the adapted and frozen zero-shot outputs, bounding adaptation damage on genuinely ambiguous inputs.
  Evaluated across 31 benchmarks—ten standard datasets, four ImageNet variants, eleven biomedical datasets, and seven remote-sensing datasets—CR-TPT achieves state-of-the-art calibration while maintaining competitive accuracy, confirming that memory-grounded, continuously routed adaptation yields both accurate and trustworthy predictions.
