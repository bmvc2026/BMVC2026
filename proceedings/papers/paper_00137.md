abstract: |-
  Diffusion-based instruction-driven image editing has shown strong performance on appearance-centric edits.
  However, it often fails when an instruction requires physical state changes, such as collision, deformation, fluid motion, or combustion.
  Recent video-prior editing methods address this problem by treating image editing as short-horizon image-to-video generation.
  In this setting, intermediate frames provide temporal cues for physical dynamics, and the final frame is used as the edited image.
  While effective, this approach is costly because denoising must be performed over multiple frames and many sampling steps.
  To reduce this cost while retaining the benefits of video priors, we propose PhysCache, a training-free residual caching strategy for efficient video-prior image editing.
  Rather than treating all denoising steps uniformly, PhysCache estimates a lightweight reuse-sensitivity score from localized residual concentration, high-frequency residual variation, and a coherent-shift discount.
  Based on this score, PhysCache makes caching more conservative when stale residual reuse may harm physics-sensitive edits, and reuses cached residuals more aggressively when reuse is likely to be safe.
  Evaluating this trade-off requires measuring whether edited images follow the intended physical behavior, but existing image editing benchmarks are not designed for this purpose.
  We therefore introduce PhysEditBench, a benchmark and VLM-based evaluation protocol for physics-sensitive instruction-based image editing.
  Experiments on multiple image-to-video backbones show that PhysCache improves the quality--latency trade-off over generic caching baselines, preserving physics-sensitive editing quality while achieving inference speedups.
