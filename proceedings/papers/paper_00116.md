title: Boundary Distance Regression and Adaptive Depth Allocation for Temporal Action Localization

- Sanjeda Akter

abstract: |-
  Temporal action localization requires precise boundaries on long untrimmed
  videos, yet most detectors score boundaries as classification peaks and spend
  near-uniform computation across time. We treat both limitations as one design
  problem driven by a single signal: local boundary uncertainty. Boundary
  Distance Regression (BDR) replaces classification-style boundary scores with
  two stride-normalized oriented distance fields, one for action starts and one
  for ends, and extracts boundaries by local zero-crossing interpolation inside
  per-boundary supervision windows; the dual-field design avoids the artifactual
  midpoint sign flips of a single nearest-boundary target and is small enough to
  retrofit into existing TAL heads. A stylized Cram\'er--Rao bound and a local
  Taylor bound explain why broad plateaus are hard to localize and how distance
  error propagates through zero-crossing interpolation. Adaptive Temporal
  Refinement (ATR) then uses BDR field reliability to allocate a continuous depth
  weight $\tau_t \in [0,1]$ along time, refining deeply near uncertain
  transitions and cheaply elsewhere. On THUMOS14 with end-to-end fine-tuning, ATR
  reaches $57.2\%$ mAP@0.7 at $214$G FLOPs and $160$ms latency, $+2.5\%$ over a
  uniform-depth baseline at $22\%$ fewer FLOPs; with a frozen backbone it obtains
  $56.5\%$ mAP@0.7 and $58.2\%$ average mAP at $151$G FLOPs, $+2.9\%$ over a
  matched uniform baseline with $24\%$ fewer FLOPs and $29\%$ lower batch-1
  latency, and the largest gain on short actions ($<2$s: $+4.2\%$). BDR alone
  improves BMN, ActionFormer, TriDet, and TemporalMaxer by $+1.8$ to $+3.1\%$
  mAP@0.7 at negligible inference cost.
