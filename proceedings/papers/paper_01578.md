abstract: |-
  The rapid progress and widespread adoption of AI-generated images have raised concerns regarding authorship, accountability, and the ability to trace a generated image (target) back to its source.
  In closed-set scenarios, where the original generator is included among the candidate generators used during training, existing attribution methods report near-perfect (up to 100\%) accuracy.
  However, open-set attribution, in which the target image may originate from a generator outside the training set, remains an open research problem and is the focus of this work.
  One key challenge in the open-set scenario is that changes in the number, diversity, or similarity of the generators used in training have been shown to result in markedly different open-set performance, even when closed-set performance remains high.
  In addition, attribution performance may also vary when faced with semantic content and visual diversity; for instance, an attribution model optimized for facial images may struggle when applied to diverse objects or scenes.
  To address these limitations, we propose Content-Based Verification for Attribution (CoVAtt), a framework designed to reduce semantic-content bias and encourage greater reliance on generator-specific cues.
  We evaluated CoVAtt under realistic open-set conditions on 13 state-of-the-art (SOTA) text-to-image generators and compared its performance to that of SOTA attribution methods,
  achieving an average AUROC improvement of 28\% and 14\% compared to Siamese Verification and OCC-CLIP, respectively, while exhibiting lower variability across random seeds and training-set configurations.
  In addition, to facilitate future research, we release the firscontent-based attribution dataset.
