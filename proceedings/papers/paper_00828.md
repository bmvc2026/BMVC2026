abstract: |-
  Forgery of identity documents and scene-text images is a growing threat. Vision-language models can detect well but cannot localize. Pixel-level forensic networks can localize but cannot explain. We close this gap with TamperLens, a tool-augmented chain-of-thought framework built on a 8B vision-language backbone. A lightweight region proposer first finds candidate areas in the image, then extracts forensic evidence (ELA, SRM, noise, contrast, frequency, colour, and per-face identity signals) from each area. The LoRA-fine-tuned backbone reads this evidence pack, writes a short chain-of-thought, and emits bounding boxes using native grounding tokens. Six forensic tools support the agent during reasoning; three of them are introduced in this work: Cross-Field Checksum Inference, Guilloché-Flow Consistency, and Template Coherence. We evaluate on two standard benchmarks. On OSTF we reach Avg F1 0.9117 and mIoU
  0.76. On FantasyID we reach Avg F1 0.8742 and mIoU 0.584. The approach uses only 8B parameters and low-rank adapters, yet it exceeds the reported GPT-4o-high baseline on both benchmarks while also producing structured forensic rationales alongside each
  prediction. This makes the model useful for both automated screening and human review.
