title: 'CoSFR: Cosine-Guided Sample-Wise Feature Restoration for Robust Zero-Shot Vision-Language Models'

abstract: |-
  Vision-Language Models (VLMs) such as CLIP provide strong zero-shot recognition
  but remain highly vulnerable to adversarial perturbations. We identify a consistent and
  measurable phenomenon in the CLIP feature space, source–anchor disparity, in which
  adversarial inputs deviate systematically further from their Gaussian-noise anchors than
  clean inputs (AUROC 0.908 on ViT-B/32 under PGD-10 at ε = 1/255, averaged over 11
  datasets). This finding exposes a common limitation shared by current training-free test-
  time defenses: they apply restoration uniformly without acknowledging that clean and
  adversarial inputs require fundamentally different treatment. Based on this observation,
  we propose Cosine-Guided Sample-Wise Feature Restoration (CoSFR), a training-free
  test-time defense that uses the source–anchor cosine distance as a sample-wise signal
  to control the restoration strength. This design enables stronger correction for highly
  deviated adversarial inputs while applying conservative updates to well-aligned clean in-
  puts, thereby improving robustness while preserving clean accuracy. We evaluate CoSFR
  through complementary studies spanning 11 datasets, three CLIP architectures, diverse
  attacks, and multiple perturbation budgets, showing an improved accuracy–robustness
  trade-off over representative adversarial fine-tuning, adversarial prompt-tuning, and training-
  free purification baselines.
