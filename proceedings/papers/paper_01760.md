abstract: |-
  Recent advances in vision foundation models (VFMs), especially the Segment Anything Model (SAM), have enabled strong promptable segmentation. However, applying SAM to unseen medical segmentation tasks still typically requires manual prompts or
  task-specific adaptation, limiting its usability in realistic clinical workflows. Existing automated prompting methods alleviate manual interaction by extracting sparse point cues from labeled reference samples, but they remain unreliable for two reasons: (1) SAM’s
  label-agnostic pretraining yields limited category-level discrimination in medical images; and (2) sparse prompts provide only local and unstable guidance, making them sensitive to visually salient but semantically irrelevant regions. To address these limitations, we
  propose DISASeg, a fully automated few-shot medical image segmentation framework that couples the semantic discrimination of DINOv2 with the spatial precision of SAM. DISASeg introduces a Frequency-aware Semantic Adapter to inject DINOv2 semantics into SAM features in the frequency domain while preserving fine-grained localization cues. It further designs a Dense Prompt Generator that establishes patch-level query-reference correspondences and produces contour-aware dense mask prompts for SAM decoding. Trained on 47 diverse medical datasets and evaluated on six unseen benchmarks, DISASeg achieves state-of-the-art one-shot performance and competitive few-shot generalization without user interaction, requiring neither manual prompting nor task-specific fine-tuning.
