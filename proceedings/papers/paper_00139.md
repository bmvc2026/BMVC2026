abstract: |-
  Label-free virtual staining aims to infer fluorescence microscopy images from bright-field observations, enabling scalable and non-destructive analysis for high-content microscopy. However, this task is challenged by the intrinsic ambiguity of fluorescence prediction from label-free inputs and the practical requirement of screening-scale throughput, which limits the applicability of deterministic models and computationally intensive diffusion methods. We address these challenges with a parameter-efficient latent diffusion framework that models conditional uncertainty while enabling single-step inference through Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) of a pretrained diffusion prior.
  By modeling fluorescence generation as a conditional distribution given bright-field observations, our method better captures the intrinsic ambiguity of label-free prediction and improves preservation of high-frequency morphology. Operating in latent space improves scalability, and LoRA restricts optimization to a small set of trainable parameters, enabling stable training and strong generalization under limited paired microscopy data and domain shifts across imaging conditions. Extensive experiments on label-free virtual staining benchmarks demonstrate consistent improvements over strong regression, GAN, and diffusion baselines across MAE and Pearson correlation, and qualitative results show sharper, more coherent reconstructions with enhanced subcellular textures and boundaries. Code and models will be made available.
