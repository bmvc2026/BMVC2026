abstract: |-
  Multi-domain training may regularise low-resource breast imaging models, but its benefits and limits remain unclear. We study this question for structured report generation across mammography (MG), ultrasound (US), contrast-enhanced spectral mammography (CESM), and magnetic resonance imaging (MR). We harmonise 12 public datasets into a common exam-centric schema and train a DINOv2--GPT-2 model that generates auditable reports as ordered decisions: modality, structured findings, and BI-RADS assessment.
  Our results show that multi-domain training improves BI-RADS macro-F1 for low-resource US and CESM while largely preserving performance for high-resource MG and MR. Hold-one-modality-out experiments show that auxiliary domains can regularise learning but cannot replace target-domain supervision, and reveal asymmetric transfer, including possible negative transfer from MR to US. Ordinal BI-RADS analysis further shows that aggregate macro-F1 hides a safety-critical failure: BI-RADS 5 cases are poorly handled, with only around 30\% predicted within $\pm1$. These findings motivate class-aware calibration, high-risk recall targets, and modality-aware evaluation for structured breast imaging report generation. Code is available at https://github.com/jddomingues-fcul/MultiBreast.
