abstract: |-
  As text-to-image generative models are deployed at scale, ensuring they cannot produce restricted, harmful, or legally protected content has become a critical safety requirement. Most concept-erasing techniques implicitly assume a static set of forbidden concepts. In real-world deployments, however, restrictions evolve due to changing policies, licensing agreements, and safety requirements, making continual erasing a practical necessity. While a few recent works have explored sequential concept removal, no standardized protocol exists for comparing continual erasure methods under consistent concept sequences, prompt sets, and evaluation metrics, leaving it unclear which methods degrade gracefully over repeated erasures and which do not. Moreover, restoration, the selective re-enabling of
  a previously erased concept without undoing other erasures, has never been formally defined
  or evaluated, despite being essential for long-term model governance. To close both gaps, we
  (i) introduce CORE-Bench, a standardized benchmark for jointly evaluating continual concept erasure and restoration across multiple domains; (ii) formally define the restoration task and propose a general constrained counter-edit formulation that instantiates selective restoration for both training-based
  and closed-form erasers; and (iii) demonstrate, through systematic benchmarking, the clear advantage of
  attention-based closed-form editors for selective restoration with minimal leakage over training-based alternatives on open-source diffusion models. Code and benchmark will be released upon acceptance.
