abstract: |-
  Flow Matching has emerged as a principled framework for cross-modal
  alignment in few-shot learning, transporting visual features toward semantic
  text prototypes via a learned continuous velocity field integrated by a
  fixed-step ODE solver. In this work we ask: \emph{can sequence models serve
  as effective proxies for FM-based alignment, replacing ODE integration with
  learned autoregressive state prediction?} We propose two architectures
  \textbf{GRUFlow} and \textbf{TransformerFlow}  that recast cross-modal
  alignment as a direct state-prediction problem over $K$ discrete trajectory
  steps, supervised by ground-truth linear interpolants at every step.
  GRUFlow employs a gated recurrent cell with AdaLN timestep conditioning;
  TransformerFlow replaces the recurrent cell with causal self-attention over
  the full trajectory history, providing richer non-Markovian context.
  On controlled synthetic transport tasks we show that when the transport
  assignment is ambiguous crossing, multi-modal, or manifold-structured
  paths  the sequence proxy resolves correspondences that a memoryless
  velocity field cannot, reducing pointwise transport error by up to
  $50\times$.
  Across 11 standard few-shot benchmarks, five PEFT backbones, and three
  shot settings, both GRUFlow (\textasciitilde 6.5\,M params) and
  TransformerFlow (\textasciitilde 9.82\,M params) \emph{match or improve
  over FMA (\textasciitilde 16.65\,M params)}  up to $+2.3\%$ at 1-shot on
  difficult datasets while using as little as $39\%$ of FMA's parameter
  budget.
  Crucially, both architectures generalise consistently across all four PEFT
  methods tested, with the strongest absolute gains on weaker feature
  extractors, confirming that non-Markovian trajectory refinement is
  architecture-agnostic and most effective precisely where residual cross-modal
  misalignment is largest.
