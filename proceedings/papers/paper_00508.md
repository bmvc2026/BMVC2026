abstract: |-
  Human video generation is becoming an increasingly important task with broad applications in graphics, entertainment, and embodied AI.
  Despite the rapid progress of video diffusion models (VDMs), controllable human video generation remains challenging.
  Text prompts alone provide weak control over articulated body motion, while common sparse signals such as keypoints or skeletons leave body structure, limb volume, and viewpoint-dependent silhouettes underconstrained. In this work, we propose Cameo, a CAscaded framework for controllable human Motion vidEO generation. It seamlessly bridges Text-to-Motion (T2M) models and conditional VDMs, mitigating suboptimal factors that may arise in this process across both training and inference through carefully designed components.
  Specifically, we analyze and prepare both textual prompts and visual conditions to effectively train the VDM, ensuring robust alignment between motion descriptions, conditioning signals, and the generated videos. Furthermore, we introduce a camera-view selection module that connects the two stages, automatically selecting viewpoints aligned with the input text to enhance coherence and reduce manual intervention. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach on both the MovieGen benchmark and a newly introduced benchmark tailored to the T2M–VDM combination, while highlighting its versatility across diverse use cases.
