abstract: |-
  Current camera-controlled novel view synthesis (NVS) methods rely heavily on the costly fine-tuning of large diffusion models. Although this approach yields high visual fidelity, such models remain vulnerable to train-test domain gaps and often suffer from generative hallucinations that compromise input faithfulness. Moreover, their immense computational demands hinder practical accessibility for both training and inference. We challenge this prevailing trend. We hypothesize that by explicitly enforcing faithfulness to the source scene, we can bypass heavy optimization while retaining competitive perceptual quality.
  To this end, we propose a training-free NVS pipeline by extending RePaint, preserving its inherent zero-shot generality at no extra GPU memory cost. Observing that directly repurposing vanilla RePaint for NVS fails to follow camera trajectories and yields over-smoothed outputs, we overcome these limitations through two inference-time interventions: (1) Latent Homography Deformation, which directly deforms latents to target viewpoints via homography to enforce camera motion compliance, and (2) Spatially Adaptive RePaint (SA-RePaint), a zero-cost RePaint extension that enhances texture fidelity without disrupting global structure.
  Experiments show substantial gains in faithfulness and camera accuracy, with competitive perceptual quality, demonstrating that high-quality NVS does not necessarily require training or heavy test-time optimization when source-scene faithfulness is explicitly enforced.
