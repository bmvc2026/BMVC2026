abstract: |-
  Autonomous driving systems often suffer from temporal misalignment across multimodal sensors. Most existing approaches address temporal misalignment from the hardware perspective, such as by synchronizing trigger times. From a software perspective,
  we formulate the problem as extrapolating object detections, including both positions and high-level latent features, from asynchronous timestamps to target time steps. Trajectory prediction, while sharing the goal of forecasting future object positions, can only partially
  solve this problem since it does not consider how the associated high-level latent features evolve over time. Methods used to improve temporal fusion transform the corresponding high-dimensional features but typically under the linear motion assumption. This
  paper proposes QTExtra, a query-based extrapolation method that extrapolates not only future positions but also high-dimensional features of the detected objects across multiple time steps. A transformer is employed to align detection queries from asynchronous
  camera frames to target time steps, making feature-level extrapolation a key capability alongside position prediction. On the nuScenes validation set, QTExtra consistently outperforms the linear motion baseline across all motion subclasses: static, linear, and
  nonlinear. The most substantial gains are achieved on nonlinear motions for the car category, where our method reduces the average position error by 15%, the orientation error by 10%, and the velocity error by 12% at the 2.0 s prediction horizon. Furthermore, QTExtra
  achieves these results without relying on high-definition maps or tracking outputs, demonstrating its effectiveness as a standalone temporal extrapolation module.
