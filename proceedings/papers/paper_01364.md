abstract: The pursuit of fair and equitable AI in human-centric tasks is critically hindered by the inherent biases and privacy risks of real-world datasets, which offer imbalanced representations of identity and often contain sensitive personal information. Consequently, state-of-the-art generative models trained on this skewed data exhibit unfair performance, particularly in identity-preserving generation. In this work we propose a pipeline for controllable generation of dataset with diverse faces for generative models training and finetuning. We achieve this by expanding foundational Arc2Face diffusion model with a flow matching generative model in the manifold of identity embeddings. We demonstrate that such approach achieves both goals of generative fidelity and label diversity not present in real human-centric datasets. As a result we collect and further contextualize a highly balanced identity-centric dataset of humans named SynthFaces. By design, our dataset is completely synthetic and contains no real individuals, thereby circumventing the privacy and ethical concerns inherent in web-scraped or user-sourced data. We purpose it for training and tuning of fair models for various downstream tasks, and showcase its usability in finetuning an existing adapter-based method for personalized generation.
